The module storyboard

The design document behind “Good digital security habits”, screen by screen: what the learner sees, what they do and how the module responds.

Project brief

Context
Internship at iLab TN, September 2026
Audience
Employees from all departments, no prerequisites
Length
10 minutes
Format
Web module, 960 × 540, 8 screens in 2 scenes
Assessment
5 questions × 10 points, 80% pass mark
Audio
No voice-over: everything is readable on screen
Player
Menu, free navigation, notes, glossary (3 terms), Cybermalveillance.gouv.fr resource
Variables
Prenom (text) · CartesVues (number) · Vu1, Vu2, Vu3 (true/false)

Learning objectives

  • IDENTIFYa phishing message from concrete clues
  • CHOOSEa strong password from several options
  • APPLYthe right sequence of actions for a suspicious email

02
Discovery

Three habits

Exploration
Goal
Discover the three core habits before the assessment.
On-screen text
“Bonjour %Prenom% ! Ouvre chaque carte.” (Hi! Open each card.) · counter “Cartes explorées : %CartesVues% / 3” · 3 cards · Next button.
Interaction
Clicking a card opens its layer, switches the card to a “✓ visited” state and adds 1 to CartesVues (once per card, using Vu1–Vu3). Next stays disabled until all 3 cards are opened.
Feedback
Passwords layer: use a long, unique passphrase for each account and turn on two-factor authentication.
Updates layer: install system and app updates quickly, they fix flaws already being exploited.
Phishing layer: be wary of urgent messages asking you to click or pay; check the sender and use the official website.
“J'ai compris” (Got it) button closes the layer.
Animation
Cards fly in from the bottom, staggered (0.2 s · 0.4 s · 0.6 s); layer panel zooms in.
Navigation
Next (after 3/3) → Assessment scene.
Design note
Exploration interaction: the learner must open all three cards to move on.

Valider
04
Assessment

Bank logo

True / false · 10 pts · 2 tries
Goal
“IDENTIFY” objective: a logo doesn't prove a message is genuine.
On-screen text
“An email showing your bank's logo is always genuine.” · True · False (correct).
Interaction
True / False, then Submit. A second attempt is allowed.
Feedback
Correct: “Right: a logo can be copied in two seconds.”
1st mistake: “Not quite. Try again.”
2nd mistake: “That's not the right answer.”
Navigation
Continue → question 3.
Design note
Trick question: the second attempt lets the learner correct their first instinct.
